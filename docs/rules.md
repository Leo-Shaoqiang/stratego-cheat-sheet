# Official Stratego Rules & Gameplay Guide

The comprehensive, quick-reference guide to the classic game of battlefield strategy.

Reference: [Stratego Rules Online](https://strategocheatsheet.com/#rules)

---

## 1. Objective of the Game

The objective of Stratego is to locate and capture the opponent's **Flag** (`F`), or to eliminate all opposing movable pieces so that the opponent has no legal moves remaining.

---

## 2. Battlefield Setup

- **Board Dimensions**: 10 columns by 10 rows (100 total squares).
- **The Lakes**: Two 2x2 water obstacles are located in the center of the board (rows 5 and 6, columns 3-4 and 7-8).
  - No piece may ever enter or cross the lakes.
  - Movement must route through the three land corridors (columns 1-2, 5-6, and 9-10).
- **Army Size**: Each player commands an army of **40 pieces**, placed in the four rows closest to them (rows 1-4 for Player 1, rows 7-10 for Player 2).
- **Fog of War**: Pieces are placed with their rank identifiers facing the player. The opponent sees only the blank backs of the pieces until combat occurs.

---

## 3. Movement Rules

1. **Turn Order**: Red moves first, followed by Blue. Players must make one move per turn.
2. **Standard Movement**: Any movable piece can move **one square** orthogonal (forward, backward, left, or right) into an adjacent empty square.
   - Diagonal movement is strictly forbidden.
3. **Scout Abilities**: Scouts (`2` in Modern, `9` in Classic) may move any number of open squares in a straight orthogonal line, similar to a Rook in chess. They cannot jump over other pieces or across the lakes.
4. **Immobile Pieces**:
   - **Bombs** (`B`) and the **Flag** (`F`) can **never** move. Once placed during setup, they remain in their squares for the duration of the match.

---

## 4. Special Movement Restrictions

- **Two-Square Rule**: A player cannot move a piece back and forth between the same two squares for more than three consecutive turns.
- **More-Square Rule**: A player cannot continuously pursue an opposing piece around a circuit of squares without breaking the pursuit.

---

## 5. Combat Resolution

When a piece moves into a square occupied by an opposing piece, an attack occurs:
1. Both players reveal the rank of the clashing pieces.
2. The piece with the **lower rank value** is defeated and removed from the board.
3. If both pieces have the **identical rank**, both pieces are defeated and removed from the board.
4. The winning piece occupies the contested square.
